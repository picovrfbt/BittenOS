The basicsGetting a face on the mask

The page has four tabs along the bottom:

MaskFaces, brightness, scrolling text and quick phrases
DesignThe face maker and the screen designer
SettingsEverything that can be configured
SystemHow the mask is doing, and the tools

Tap a face tile to show it. Tiles show half of each face at double size, because nearly every face is a mirror image; a face whose halves genuinely differ is shown whole.

The two round buttons at the top right are sleep (the moon) and displays off (the power symbol). Displays off darkens the panels while everything else keeps running, so it comes straight back.

Every setting saves the moment you change it. There is no save button, and if the mask refuses a change the control goes back to what the mask actually has.
BoopsSetting up the sensor so it triggers properly

The boop sensor uses two things at once: how close something is, and whether it casts a shadow. A stray closeness reading can't darken the light sensor, so demanding both is what stops random boops.

Setting it up, in Settings > Boop:

  1. With nothing near the visor, press Calibrate. Closeness should sit near 0.
  2. Hold your hand on the snout and watch Closeness. Set Boop distance about halfway between resting and your hand.
  3. In normal light your hand should push Shadow past its yellow line. If not, lower Shadow needed.
  4. Switch Boop on and pick the Boop face.

A boop needs closeness over the line for Readings to confirm checks in a row - five by default, about 210 ms - plus a shadow within the last second. Lower it for a quicker boop at the cost of the odd false one, raise it if the mask boops itself. In the dark there's nothing to shade, so it falls back to closeness alone and the Shadow bar greys out.

Milestones: every hundredth boop plays a face of your choosing instead of the usual one. Set the count and the face under Boop counter, or set the count to 0 to turn it off.

If boops fire at random, check Confirm with light is on. If real boops say "no shadow — ignored", lower Shadow needed.
MicrophoneMaking the mouth move with your voice

Loudness is measured peak to peak — the loudest reading minus the quietest, many times a second — so there is no resting level to drift and nothing to get stuck on.

  1. Settings > Microphone, switch Speech on and stay quiet for a second while it measures the room.
  2. Press Mic test: keep quiet for two seconds, then talk for three. Quiet should be a small number and talking several times bigger.
  3. Press Measure loud and talk normally for three seconds. That sets Range so ordinary speech lands about 80 out of 100.

Mouth movement chooses how the mouth reacts. Shapes opens it wider the louder you are, in four steps. Flap is the original single-pixel opening.

Trigger point is the level that counts as talking — the line on the meter. Raise it if the mouth moves when the room is merely noisy.

If the meter says No signal, the mask isn't hearing the mic at all: check its wiring rather than any setting.
BrightnessManual, or set from the light around you

The slider on the Mask tab sets panel brightness, 0 to 15. With auto-brightness on it locks and shows Auto instead.

Auto-brightness reads the light sensor. The panels shine into that sensor, so a plain reading partly measures the panels themselves, which is what the three methods are about:

BlankSwitches the panels off for a quarter second at each check and reads the room. Most accurate, brief blink.
HybridWatches the reading with the panels on and only blanks when the light seems to have changed. Few blinks.
LiveNever blinks; the panels' own glow nudges it brighter.

Calibrating: in the darkest place you'll wear it press Measure, then Use now on Dark point. Repeat in bright light for Bright point. Lowest and Highest cap the range.

Keep Highest modest if the panels run from the board's 5V pin — full brightness draws far more than the few watts it uses at 5.
SleepDozing off, and waking up

The moon button sleeps immediately. For it to happen on its own, switch Auto sleep on and set Sleep after to the minutes of stillness to wait. Stillness comes from the tilt sensor, so this needs the BMI270.

Moving the mask wakes it, and so does picking any face. Wake sensitivity is how much movement counts — raise it if it wakes on its own, lower it if it ignores you.

Deep sleep is separate and off by default: after the minutes you set, the panels switch off entirely. Any movement or a button press brings them back.

Wake-up flicker is how they come back from a deep sleep: instead of all fourteen panels snapping on together, each one stutters on in its own time, dim and unsteady before it settles, like an old device warming up. Old CRT is slow with a heavy stutter, Fluorescent tube is fast and twitchy. Picture tearing scrambles a few rows while it warms up, and Warm-up time sets how long the whole thing takes. Press Play to watch it without waiting for a sleep. Moving the mask, pressing the button or picking a face cuts it short.

Flicker on startup does the same thing when you switch the mask on, so it boots like an old device coming to life. The panels stay dark until the face is loaded, then warm up over the top of it.

Loading bar on startup draws a framed bar along both maws and fills it, and only then does the flicker bring the face in. The frame appears whole from the start and the fill grows outward from the nose on both sides at once, jumping and pausing rather than creeping, so it reads as something working. Loading bar time sets how long it takes, separately from Warm-up time, so the two halves of the startup can be tuned against each other. Mouth bob leaves the bar alone. Switch either off on its own — with the bar off, startup is just the flicker as before.

WiFiThe mask's own network, and joining yours

The mask always runs its own network, named and secured in Settings > The mask's own WiFi. Connecting a phone to it works anywhere, but that network has no internet behind it, so while your phone is on it you lose data, and calls that go over the internet stop working.

Join a network fixes that. Switch it on and give the mask your phone's hotspot or your home WiFi, and it connects to that instead — your phone keeps its own connection and its internet, and still reaches the mask. The mask's own network keeps running the whole time, so a wrong password here can never lock you out.

Once it has joined, Status shows the address to type into a browser, and the mask also answers to the name you set plus .local (protogen.local by default). You can put the address on the status screen too: add a Live value element set to IP address in the screen designer, and the mask will show you where to find it.

One quirk worth knowing: the mask has a single radio, so when it joins your network its own network moves to that network's channel. Anything already connected to the mask's own network has to connect again. That is the hardware, not a fault.

BluetoothControlling the mask without touching WiFi

Switch Remote (BLE) on in Settings and the mask advertises over Bluetooth under the same name as its WiFi. This is the one way to control it that leaves your phone's own connection completely alone — no joining anything, no losing internet.

It works with the physical ProtoESP remote, and with a phone. Commands are a letter, a colon and a value, written to characteristic ffe1 on service ffe0:

s:Ask what the mask is doing — face, brightness, boops, sleeping, displays, speech
f:nameSwitch to a face, e.g. f:angry
b:0-15Panel brightness
d:0 / d:1Displays off or on
z:0 / z:1Wake or sleep
p:0-5Play a quick phrase
n:1Next screen page
w:1Play the wake-up warm-up
m:0 / m:1Speech off or on

You can try all of this today with a free app like nRF Connect before any custom app exists — connect, find ffe1, and write s: to see the mask answer.

DinoA game on the maw

On the System tab, beside the diagnostics, is a Start button. Press it and both maws become a playfield: a dinosaur runs along the bottom and cacti come in from the back of the head toward the snout. The game plays on one maw and is mirrored onto the other, so it reads as deliberate rather than lopsided.

Jumping: while a game is running the mask's own button jumps, whatever it is normally set to, and goes back to its usual job the moment the game ends. There is a Jump button on the page too, for when the mask is not on your head — though a phone tap has to cross WiFi, so the button on the mask is the responsive one.

The score counts distance and your best is remembered. Crash and it flashes, shows the wreck for a moment, then puts your face back.

It takes the panels over completely while it runs, so the face, mouth bob and blinking all stand aside until it finishes. Press Stop to end it early.

The buttonShort press, double press and hold

Each of Short press, Double press and Hold can be set separately in Settings > Button:

NothingIgnore it
Next / Previous faceStep through the faces
Displays on/offDarken the panels, everything else keeps running
Sleep / wakeSame as the moon button
A chosen faceJump straight to one face
A quick phraseScroll a saved message
Next screen pageStep the little status screen

Hold for sets how long a hold takes, 1.5 seconds by default.

Setting Double press to anything makes a single press wait about a quarter second to see whether a second follows. Leave it on Nothing and a single press acts the instant you let go.
Scrolling text and quick phrasesSaying something without a keyboard

Scrolling text on the Mask tab types a one-off message, up to 64 characters. Pick a speed and whether it repeats or plays once, then press Show. It scrolls across the mouth while the eyes keep the current face.

Quick phrases are saved messages, one tap each. Press Edit, fill in up to six with their own speed and repeat setting, then Save.

The mask builds phrases itself rather than the phone doing it, which is why a phrase set on the button works with no phone anywhere near you.
FacesChoosing, managing and drawing them

Manage on the Faces card adds Rename and Delete to every tile. Two faces are protected: default, which loads at startup, and whichever face is showing — switch away first to delete that one.

Face maker on the Design tab draws faces pixel by pixel. Upload faces and Download faces take a single .json or a .zip of them.

Transitions dip the panels dark between faces rather than cutting. Mouth bob lifts the mouth a pixel every second or so on any face.

Blinks appears on the Mask tab only while a face the mask can blink is showing. Most faces are animations that blink themselves; the mask can only take over on a still face. In the face maker, tick The mask may blink this face and you never need to draw blink frames.

The mask's blink takes about 450 ms, the same as the blink the default face draws for itself, so the two read as the same gesture. Double blink sets how many blinks come as a quick pair instead of one on its own — a second blink a tenth of a second behind the first. A single blink on a timer reads as a machine ticking however random the gap; the occasional pair is what makes it look alive. Default is a quarter of them; 0 turns pairs off.

Deleting or renaming a face that a setting points at leaves that setting pointing at nothing — the page warns you, so pick another.
TiltA face for each way you tilt your head

With Tilt faces on, tipping your head plays a face and going back to normal puts the old one back. There are three positions besides idle: sideways, the other way, and up, each with its own face.

Teaching it: open Calibrate positions, hold your head in each position and press Read beside it, then Save positions. The Read buttons work even with Tilt switched off. Tilting the other way has to be taught separately rather than worked out from the first - one axis changes sign, so it is a different reading, not a mirror image.

Tolerance is how close your head has to be to a taught position. Too small and it never triggers; too large and two positions overlap and it picks whichever it tests first. If a tilt face sticks, it gives up after 8 seconds and goes back on its own.

This is also where stillness for auto sleep comes from, so the tilt sensor has to be found for sleep to work at all - even with tilt faces off.

The status screenThe little screen inside the helmet

Switch it on in Settings > Status screen and pick a brightness. Upside down turns the picture round for how it's mounted.

Screen designer on the Design tab lays it out: drag things around, and each element can show always or only in a certain state.

Microphone widgets: add a Bar set to Mic level and tick Marker — the bar fills with how loud you are and the marker line sits exactly where the mouth starts moving, so you can see how close you are to setting it off. For a light that comes on when you talk, add an Icon set to mic with While the mouth is moving, and put a second one set to micOff in the same spot with While quiet — the hollow mic fills in as you speak.

Boop milestones: set Every N boops in Settings and each Nth boop plays a special face. Give it a page too: add one set to take over On a boop milestone, with a linger of a second or two, and the screen celebrates alongside the face. Put a Live value showing Boop count on it and it reads like a scoreboard.

Pages: up to eight, chosen from the bar above the screen. A page in the rotation is stepped by a button set to Next screen page, or automatically if you set a rotation time. A page that takes over appears whenever its condition holds — booped, sleeping, awake, displays off — and lingers for a moment after.

Clock: add a Live value element set to Clock, and am / pm beside it if you want it. The mask has no battery-backed clock and no internet, so it cannot know the time by itself — whichever phone opens this page tells it, and it counts on from there.

Until a phone has opened the page, the clock blinks 00:00. It forgets the time on every restart, so open the page once after powering up.
Saving from the designer updates the screen straight away. No flashing needed to change what it shows.
UpdatingNew firmware over WiFi

System > Tools > Update firmware. Choose firmware.bin or littlefs.bin; the page works out which it is and refuses anything else.

firmware.binThe program. Goes into the spare slot and only takes over once it has fully arrived. If it fails to run, the mask goes back to the previous one by itself.
littlefs.binThe faces and web pages. Keeps your settings, calibration and boop count.

Keep the page open until it says done.

When something is wrongThe usual causes
Boops fire on their ownConfirm with light off, or Boop distance too low. Recalibrate with nothing near the visor.
Boops never fireBoop distance too high, or Shadow needed too strict. Watch the meter while you touch the snout.
Mouth doesn't moveRun Mic test. "No signal" means wiring; a small difference between quiet and talking means the level settings.
Mouth moves on its ownRaise Trigger point, or press Measure silence somewhere quiet.
A sensor says not foundCheck I2C devices on the System tab: 0x39 boop, 0x68/0x69 tilt, 0x3c screen. The mask keeps looking, so a fixed wire is picked up within seconds.
Panels are darkDisplays may be off — press the power button. If auto-brightness is on in a dark room, Lowest may be 0.
Page won't loadMake sure you're on the mask's WiFi with mobile data off.

The mask keeps a log at /log if something needs a closer look.
